How Much Does a Website Cost in Dubai?

An honest look at market prices, what moves the number and where my studio sits.

Everyone asks this, and it’s usually the first question. Before “what’s your process” or “can I see your work”, it’s straight to: how much? I understand. You’re running a business, you need to budget, and too many agencies make you fill in a form and wait days for a PDF that still doesn’t give you a number.

So here is an honest answer, from someone who has been quoting websites in Dubai since 2015. First what the market looks like, then what moves the price, then where my studio sits.

Why the range is so wild

Search “website cost Dubai” and you’ll find quotes from AED 500 to AED 500,000. That isn’t helpful on its own. The reason is simple: “website” can mean a hundred different things. A template with your logo on it is a website. So is a custom platform with user accounts, online payments and a content system your team updates every day. Same word, completely different products.

It’s a bit like asking how much a car costs. The honest answer starts with “what do you need it to do?”

These are rough starting points you’ll see across Dubai, from freelancers and small studios up to large agencies. They’re market context, not my prices, and every provider counts differently.

Notice the words “at the budget end”. Those figures usually mean a lighter build, a theme underneath or fewer rounds of design. A custom site designed from scratch costs more than the floor of the market, and it should.

What affects the price

Whoever you hire, a few things move the number:

  • Number of pages and sections. More pages means more design and more building. A dental clinic with five treatments needs less than a hotel with rooms, restaurants, events and offers.
  • Custom features. Bookings, online payments, member areas, property listings with filters, multi-language content. Anything beyond static pages adds work.
  • Content. Do you have your text and photos ready, or do they need to be written and shot? Copywriting and photography are usually quoted separately.
  • Brand. If you don’t have a logo and visual identity yet, that’s a separate job, and it’s worth doing first. See brand identity.
  • Languages. In Dubai, English and Arabic is common. A second language, especially one that reads right to left, is real design and development work, not just translation.
  • Animations and interactions. Subtle motion adds polish, but getting it right takes time.
  • Tight deadlines. If it has to go live for a specific date, say so early. Squeezing a project usually costs more.

The cheap option trap

You can get a website for AED 500. Someone will take a template, change the colours, add your logo and hand it over. It will look like exactly that, and it will look like hundreds of other sites. In 2026 the same is true of sites spat out by AI builders in minutes.

I regularly meet business owners who paid twice: once for the cheap version that didn’t bring in a single enquiry, and again to do it properly. That isn’t saving money. It’s spending double. A website isn’t really an expense. It’s often the first thing a client sees before they call you, and that first impression decides whether they call at all.

“You don’t get a second first impression.”

Hidden costs to ask about

The build price isn’t the whole picture. Before you compare quotes, ask each provider about:

  • Domain and hosting. Who pays, how much per year, and whose name are they in?
  • Monthly fees. Some providers add maintenance or “platform” fees for sites that barely need them.
  • Licences. Paid themes, plugins, fonts and stock photos can carry yearly costs.
  • Changes after launch. What does it cost to add a page or update prices six months from now?
  • Ownership. If you leave, do you take the code and content with you?
