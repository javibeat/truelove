Web Design in the Age of AI: Why Handmade Still Wins

Anyone can generate a website now, and that’s exactly the problem.

Let me get something out of the way: I use AI every day. It helps me write code, test ideas and catch mistakes faster. Anyone in this industry who says they don’t is either not being straight with you or falling behind. So this isn’t one of those “AI is ruining everything” articles.

But something has happened to the web over the last couple of years, and if you’re about to invest in a website for your business, you should understand it.

Everything started to look the same

Today anyone can type a sentence into an AI website builder and have a working site in a few minutes. A huge number of people have done exactly that. What the demo videos don’t mention is that those sites look like each other. Same layouts, same gradients, same rounded cards, same big headline with the same three feature boxes underneath. The tools learned from the same patterns, so they repeat the same patterns.

People have a name for this now: AI slop. Your customers may not use the term, but they feel it. They land on a page and something whispers “template”. And a template says: this business is interchangeable with every other one. That’s a problem whether you run a dental clinic, a boutique hotel, a law firm or an online shop.

What that did to the market

Here’s the paradox. When everyone can generate a website, a generated website stops being worth much. Simply having a site no longer says anything about you, the same way having an email address stopped impressing anyone a long time ago.

What became scarce is distinctiveness. A site that could only belong to you. A site where the typography, the pace, the images and the words feel like the company behind it. That was always the point of design, but for twenty years you could get away with decent and generic. Not any more. Decent and generic is now free, instant and everywhere.

“When everyone has a website, the thing worth paying for is a website that couldn’t belong to anyone else.”

And now AI reads your site too

There’s a second shift that matters just as much. Your website isn’t only read by people any more. When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude or Google’s AI Overviews for “a good family dentist near me” or “a property agent who handles off-plan in Dubai”, those tools read websites to build their answer.

A generated site with vague copy and a stock photo of a smiling team gives them almost nothing to work with. A site written by someone who understands your business, with clear pages for what you offer, who it’s for and where you are, gives them something to quote. Handmade, here, doesn’t mean decorative. It means considered: the structure and the words are decided by someone who knows what your customers ask. More on that in How to Get Found When People Ask AI Instead of Google.

What AI is genuinely good at

Credit where it’s due. In my studio, AI has changed how I work:

  • Speed. Repetitive technical work goes much faster than it used to. That saving goes into the parts of the project that need a person.
  • Exploration. I can try several directions in the time it used to take to build one. Bad ideas die sooner, and the good one gets more attention.
  • Quality checks. Testing, accessibility checks (making sure everyone can use the site, including people with poor eyesight or who use screen readers), speed audits. The boring, important work gets done more thoroughly.

Notice what isn’t on that list: taste. Deciding what a brand should feel like. Knowing when a page needs less, not more. Sitting with a client, whether that’s a wellness coach, a photographer, a restaurant owner or a startup founder, and understanding what makes them different before a single pixel exists. AI carries out instructions. It doesn’t care. Caring is the job.

How to tell the difference when you’re buying

If you’re hiring someone to build your site, the question is no longer “can they build a website?” Everyone can. The questions that matter now:

  • Does their portfolio look like one template in different colours? If every project has the same layout, you’re looking at a template operation. Human or AI, it doesn’t matter.
  • Do they ask about your business before talking about pages? Design decisions should come from who you are and who your customers are, not from whatever the tool suggests first.
  • Can they explain why, not just what? Anyone can show you a nice page. Ask why the text is that size, why the page opens that way, why the button says what it says. If there’s no answer, there was no thinking.
  • Is it built for you, or assembled for you? A site built by hand, without templates or page builders, can be shaped exactly around your content. One assembled from ready-made blocks has to squeeze your business into someone else’s shape.

Where I’ve landed

I’ve been designing websites for more than ten years, and I’ve never been more sure about the value of doing it by hand. Not by hand as in refusing the tools. By hand as in every decision passes through a person who knows your name, has heard your story, and would be embarrassed if the result looked like everyone else’s.

That’s how every website we make is built: no templates, no page builders, and no timeline pulled out of a hat. How long it takes depends on the design and the scope, and we agree it together after the first conversation.

AI made average free. It made remarkable more valuable than it has ever been. That isn’t a threat to good design. It’s the best argument for it.
